Engineering Lab

Engineering begins with understanding a problem, identifying its underlying principles and developing a systematic approach to evaluating possible solutions. The Erry Mobility Engineering Lab connects biomedical science, mathematical modelling and assistive design through three examples: reaching a key, fitting a keyguard and explaining balance.

Turn a design idea into a calculation

Each example starts with an engineering question and identifies what the calculation can tell us. All numbers below are illustrative inputs.

How far is the same key from a fixed start point?

Set one sideways offset and two forward offsets. Keep the start point, target key, lateral offset and keyboard orientation fixed. A smaller straight-line distance is a geometric result. The real hand path, joint loading, comfort and typing performance require separate observations.

Setup A: 200.0 mm. Setup B: 150.0 mm. Change (B − A): −50.0 mm.
Show the equation and worked example

On a flat desk, distance d = √(x² + y²), where x is sideways offset and y is forward offset in millimetres. Change = dB − dA.

Setup A uses 120 and 160 mm, giving √40,000 = 200 mm. Setup B uses 120 and 90 mm, giving √22,500 = 150 mm. The modelled distance decreases by 50 mm, or 25% of Setup A.

Will a keyguard opening clear the keycap?

Start with the smallest aperture and largest keycap cross-section expected at the guard plane. Check the worst edge gap on both axes. An opening can clear the keycap on paper and still interfere with key travel or shift in use.

Show the one-axis clearance check

For rectangular edges, the tighter-side gap = (opening width − keycap width) ÷ 2 − |centre offset|. A 17.0 mm opening around a 15.0 mm cap gives 1.0 mm per side when centred. With a 0.4 mm offset the gaps become 0.6 mm and 1.4 mm. A zero or negative result flags contact or overlap in this simplified section.

Repeat on the second axis and along full key travel. Account for variation in keycap profile, fabrication, mounting and flex before deciding whether to fabricate. Verify on the intended keyboard with the fitted, exploded and sectional drawings as planning aids.

Where is a point relative to the base of support?

For a rectangular contact region, check the shortest distance from the projected centre of mass to each edge. This compares positions in a static diagram.

Show a signed edge-distance example

For a 300 × 200 mm example rectangle with one corner at (0, 0), take the minimum of x, 300 − x, y and 200 − y. At (100, 70) mm the minimum is +70 mm and the projection is inside. At (320, 70) mm it is −20 mm and the projection is outside. Zero places it on the boundary.

This quantity is an edge margin for a rectangle. It ignores momentum, changing contacts, external forces and individual responses. It supplies no safety threshold.

Prototype architecture

The typing prototype illustrates how a browser turns interaction into a local measurement.

  1. Input
    Physical keys or on-screen controls.
  2. Events
    Observe text and editing actions.
  3. Measurement
    Calculate time and character edit distance.
  4. Storage
    Keep session trials in memory.
  5. Visualisation
    Show exact values and bars on matching scales.

Visitors describe any physical adjustment because the browser cannot observe keyboard position.

Evaluate one change at a time

Question. For this person and this short message, what changes when the same keyboard moves closer?

Requirements and checks. Document the change in position, keep the keys and keyboard orientation the same, and check access to the target and nearby controls. Reuse the message and input method. Record task completion time, final text errors and optional comfort separately.

Experimental design and data flow

Repeat comparable trials where appropriate, allow rest, and alternate which setup goes first to reduce order effects. Compare only trials with matching recorded conditions and the same Setup B change. Note practice and fatigue. Discuss trade-offs separately, such as shorter time with more errors.

The user records the setup. The software subtracts explicit paused time from the elapsed start-to-finish time and calculates final text errors as insertions, deletions and substitutions. Example: 12.0 seconds elapsed with 2.5 seconds paused gives 9.5 seconds of task time. Changing tap to top is one substitution. Correction actions are logged separately from final errors. Finished records stay in the session unless the visitor saves a browser-local journal entry or exports CSV.

How the scoring algorithm works. The browser compares successive prefixes of the sample and the entered text. For each pair, it keeps the lowest cost from three possibilities: add one character, remove one, or substitute one. Matching characters add zero cost. Comparing an empty prefix with a prefix of length n costs n. The last cell gives the final edit distance, and the implementation keeps only the current and previous rows.

Evidence status

Software tests check that the tools behave as designed. Erry Mobility has no participant dataset or aggregate performance findings. Keyguard fit, ergonomic benefit and individual balance responses remain unverified.

Limitations

Erry Mobility is an educational assistive technology project. It has no established clinical effectiveness and provides no diagnosis, personalised rehabilitation, or substitute for occupational therapy or professional assessment. It is an educational web prototype and has not been developed as a medical device. Personal experience informed the design and represents one perspective.

Device differences, fatigue, practice, familiarity, and trial order can affect results. Browser editing events cannot identify every corrected error or distinguish an intentional edit from an accidental key press. A single English sample covers a narrow task. Suitability and usability need further evaluation with intended users.

Planned experiments and future ideas

A proposed usability evaluation could ask whether users can select a setup, complete a trial, and interpret the results independently. With appropriate oversight and accessible consent, a later study could compare repeated trials in both orders, allowing rest and documenting the equipment and chosen changes. Future work could explore affordable keyguard designs and additional input methods.
