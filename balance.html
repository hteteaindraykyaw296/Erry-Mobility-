Centre of mass & support contacts

  1. Find the contact points. The dark shapes represent contact with the ground.
  2. Find the support region. The pale area represents the space across those contacts in this example.
  3. Find the projection. The amber dot represents a point vertically below the centre of mass. Move it to compare with the drawn support region.

The diagram compares geometry. It does not calculate forces, motion, support from a person, or the chance of falling.

Adjust contacts, projection, and width

Contact points, support forces and pressure are schematic. Seat support, foot placement, wheel contacts, brakes, muscle responses, friction and acceleration are not calculated.
