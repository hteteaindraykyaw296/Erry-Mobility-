# Erry Mobility — Forward, your way

## Project and motivation
Erry Mobility is an applicant-led, AI-assisted educational website inspired by Erry’s lived experience of cerebral palsy. It explores access to everyday activities such as typing, writing, drawing and handling objects. Erry is pursuing biomedical engineering education and scholarship support to develop the knowledge needed to investigate assistive solutions rigorously.

## Vision
A world where movement is not a barrier to opportunity.

We envision an inclusive society in which individuals with diverse motor abilities have equitable opportunities to participate in everyday life with dignity, confidence and independence.

## Mission
Bridging human needs and engineering innovation.

Our mission is to advance understanding of human movement and assistive technology through accessible biomedical education, interactive engineering demonstrations and the exploration of affordable, person-centred adaptations.

## Goal
Turning understanding into accessibility.

To explore how biomedical engineering can address everyday barriers to participation by developing, evaluating and communicating practical assistive design concepts that respond to diverse functional needs.

## Strategic objectives
1. **Advance biomedical understanding**. Make human anatomy, motor control, biomechanics and movement-related conditions more understandable through scientifically grounded visual education.
2. **Promote accessible innovation**. Explore affordable and practical assistive design concepts that accommodate diverse movement abilities and everyday needs.
3. **Encourage evidence-informed engineering**. Apply systematic observation, measurement, prototype evaluation and transparent reporting to investigate assistive design concepts.
4. **Foster dignity and inclusion**. Challenge misconceptions about physical disability and promote a greater understanding of individual abilities, autonomy and participation.
5. **Build a foundation for future research**. Establish an educational and technical foundation for the future development of accessible, adaptable and user-centred biomedical technologies.

## Working prototype
- Evidence-linked CP awareness, prevention and rehabilitation information.
- Twelve captioned schematic 3D guides, with written alternatives and adjustable playback speed.
- Interactive, simplified 3D character, knee, ankle and hand models with structure labels and recorded-video fallbacks.
- Typing Setup Lab with repeated fixed-sample trials, elapsed time, final text errors, correction actions, optional comfort feedback, and local CSV export.
- Untimed large-key typing practice and a drawing pad with pointer and keyboard access.
- An optional, browser-local personal journal and backup import/export.
- A public project story describing engineering questions and limitations.

## Scope of the study and current limitations
The current project comprises educational content, interface development and functional checks, informed by lived experience and selected published sources. No participant study or clinical trial has been conducted. The proposed research scope is a bounded feasibility and usability study of an adaptation chosen with intended users.

One proposed study question is how elapsed time, final text errors, and comfort vary between a visitor’s usual keyboard setup and a single chosen change. The intended users, access methods, outcomes and comparison would be defined with people with CP and an occupational therapist. Appropriate oversight, accessible consent, rest options and a data-handling plan would be needed before participant research.

## Connection to biomedical engineering
The engineering framework is to define a person’s priorities, understand movement and task demands, design an adaptation, select and verify meaningful measurements, then evaluate and revise. Biomedical engineering education would provide foundations in biomechanics, motor control, instrumentation, signal processing, materials, human factors and statistics. Supervised practical work and collaboration with users and clinicians would support responsible development and interpretation of results.

Physical devices would require supervised testing and, where appropriate, professional assessment and fitting. A sensor would be considered only for a defined question, with calibration, uncertainty and privacy addressed. No sensors or therapeutic hardware are connected to this site.

## Limitations
No participant outcomes, treatment effects or improvements in emotional wellbeing have been established. Content and models have not been clinically reviewed. Simplified animations do not model individual neuromuscular control, tissue forces, balance or equipment fit, and cannot diagnose CP or prescribe a safe movement or load. Practice and self-reports are not clinical assessments of recovery. Weight and BMI are optional context with interpretive limitations.

Erry’s experience does not represent all people with CP. Inclusive participant evaluation and physical-device or varied assistive-technology testing remain future work. The references are a selected educational source set, not a systematic review. External research does not establish this site’s effectiveness.

Journal entries are self-reported and stored unencrypted in the current browser without synchronisation. Shared devices may expose entries, and clearing browser data or changing browser/domain can make records unavailable. Export/import provides a manual backup option. The site does not provide individual treatment, counselling, emergency support or a moderated peer-support service.

## Proposed pathway
1. Co-design priorities with people with varied CP experiences and seek therapist review.
2. Define one bounded engineering question and meaningful outcome.
3. Prototype under suitable supervision, including accessibility and safety checks.
4. Seek appropriate oversight and accessible consent before participant evaluation.
5. Report uncertainty and negative findings, revise, and assess affordability and access.
